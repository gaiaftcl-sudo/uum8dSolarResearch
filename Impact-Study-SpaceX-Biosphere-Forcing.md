## The measured record — what one operator has already done

- **In 2019, SpaceX accounted for approximately none of the catalogued mass reentering Earth's atmosphere. In 2025, it accounted for 45.8% of it** — nearly half of all human-made reentering mass, from zero, in six years. (MEASURED — GCAT catalogue, exact rows, `reproduce/operator-reentry-ledger.swift`. The scope is artificial mass: against the *natural* meteoric flux, all human injection combined is currently 3.1–6.3%, and this page never claims otherwise.)
- **About one in ten larger stratospheric aerosol particles already contains spacecraft metal** (VERIFIED quote — Murphy et al. 2023, *PNAS*).
- **About 460 tonnes of spacecraft dry mass (range 391–531) reentered the atmosphere in 2025; of the ablation products, roughly 45 to 61 tonnes of alumina reaches the stratosphere each season** — just under half of the catalogued mass from this operator's replacement cycle (45.8% in 2025). (MEASURED — `reproduce/mass-uncertainty-band.swift` and `reproduce/operator-reentry-ledger.swift`; the band is honest because 75.6% of the catalogued mass carries the catalogue's own estimate flag.)
- **Human metal injection currently runs at 3.1–6.3% of the natural meteoric flux** — the material the stratosphere has received for the whole history of life. (MEASURED — residence time cancels, so the burden ratio is a pure flux ratio.)

None of that is a projection. It has already happened, and no law required anyone to record any of it.

## The harm to humanity, stated by grade

**What is already measured, in bodies:** the 2026 humid-heat season put **41,174 people in ambulances in one month in Japan, 79 confirmed dead**, while Korea recorded **42.5 °C**, the highest in 122 years of observation. That harm is not attributed to satellites — it is on this wiki ([Study 28](Study-28-Wet-Bulb-Threshold-Court)) because it is the measured precedent for what an atmospheric change *feels like when it arrives*: as a shifted statistic, graded by operational instruments whose own documentation licenses verdict-sized error. The atmosphere is already killing people at the margin, and the instruments deciding when to warn them are already too coarse. **That is the system the reentry flux perturbs.**

**Established mechanism, unmeasured magnitude** — every chain below is textbook science; none has been measured against this forcing, because the forcing itself is not measured:

- Ozone loss raises surface UV-B → **skin cancer and cataract rates shift across whole populations, over decades** — a harm that arrives as statistics, never as an event anyone can point to.
- **Crop yields respond** to UV-B at percent scale.
- **Phytoplankton** — the base of the marine food web, the primary oceanic carbon sink, a large share of oxygen production — are UV-sensitive, and damage there **feeds back into the greenhouse account this same ledger measures.**
- **The polar vortex responds** — and this comes from the *reassuring* paper: Maloney 2025, the study that finds a weaker ozone hole, also reports ~1.5 K anomalies and a ~10% vortex-wind reduction at a 10 Gg/yr flux. A weakened vortex is mid-latitude weather: cold-air outbreaks, disrupted seasons.

**The planet has run this experiment once, small:** the 2019–20 Australian fires put ~1 Tg of aerosol into the stratosphere by the same mechanism class now claimed for alumina. Observed: reactive chlorine enhanced, HCl 50–60% below climatology (REPORTED — Santee et al. 2022, satellite MLS). Modelled from those observations: **3–5% southern mid-latitude ozone loss** (REPORTED — Solomon et al., *Nature* 615, 2023). Not nothing. Not collapse. **A few percent from one transient event** — and the filed constellations are a tap left running, not an eruption decaying away.

**NOT_KNOWN, and refused a number:** the phytoplankton dose-response at these forcings, the food-web and oxygen transfer functions, and the *sign* of both the ozone and radiative responses, on which the published models disagree. The court answers `NOT_KNOWN` on these and excludes them from every total. The exclusions cut in different directions, and the page says which: **the feedback couplings, if quantified, would add rather than subtract — with respect to them the totals are a floor. The contested signs could move the ozone and radiative rows either way, and no floor is claimed over them.**

## The narrow-study defence, and why it does not work

The strongest argument for doing nothing goes like this: *the published studies disagree, some find no harm, one finds benefit — so the science is unsettled, and unsettled science is no basis for regulation.*

That argument is in circulation right now, and it deserves a precise answer rather than a dismissive one, because the studies it leans on are real, peer-reviewed, and carried on this very page as evidence in the operator's favour.

**The answer is that every one of those studies is an instrument pointed at one axis of a multi-axis system, and a disagreement between narrow instruments is not a verdict about the system.** Look at what the record actually shows:

- **Each study models one axis and is silent on the rest.** The biosphere column is empty in all five. No two of them share enough axes to even be added together. A study that never modelled the ocean food web cannot find it safe — it can only fail to mention it, and *silence transmitted as reassurance is the exact mechanism by which a narrow result becomes a general one.* "No effect on the axis I modelled" gets quoted as "no effect" by the time it reaches a licensing decision.
- **Their disagreements are demonstrably arithmetic, not atmospheric.** The field's famous "28× divergence" dissolved to 3.2× when two papers' quantities were put in one set of units — no model run required. Barker's own model returns **+6.47 mW/m² under one forcing convention and −6.40 under the other**, from identical inputs: one instrument, two conventions, opposite signs. The global means every group reports are **twelve times kinder** than the regional worst case the ozone record actually produced. None of that is the atmosphere disagreeing with itself. It is the continuous-arithmetic pipeline shearing — the same floating-point, convention-dependent, average-first machinery whose vendors document, in their own manuals, that it does not reproduce across platforms.
- **A probabilistic treatment cannot be conservative about an axis it marginalised out.** Averaging over spatial gradients, ensemble members, and parameter priors produces a smooth curve *on the modelled axis* — and says exactly nothing about the coupled axes that were never in the state vector. The smoothness is a property of the method, not of the planet.

So the honest statement of the situation is not "the science is unsettled, relax." It is:

> **The instruments disagree with each other on sign, for reasons traceable to their own arithmetic; none of them models the coupled system; and the one account that holds every axis at once shows three of them moving together at the already-licensed scale. Divergence between narrow instruments is not evidence of safety. It is evidence that the instruments shear — and it is the reason to measure, not the reason to wait.**

That is also why this programme computes in exact integers, on the complete account, with the unmeasured couplings named rather than averaged away. Not because exactness is elegant — because **when instruments cannot be reconciled, the loudest institution wins by default**, and the sky should not be governed by default.

## The question before the comparison

Every bullet in the next section compares this operator favourably *within* an activity. **Public flourishing as the first principle imposes an ordering the comparisons must not be allowed to skip:**

> **1. Is the service needed? 2. Is it deliverable without the atmospheric flux? 3. Only for the remainder that survives both questions do the harm comparisons apply.**

A cleaner propellant answers *which rocket*. It silently concedes *whether the rocket* — and a slightly safer version of an unnecessary activity is harm reduction, not engineering. The substitutability question is answerable per service, with grades:

- **Orbital AI compute: fully substitutable.** Ground data centres perform this workload today; the orbital filing is an energy-economics bet, not a capability the ground lacks. For this service the propellant comparison is moot — **the flux itself is the choice.** (MEASURED against the alternative: the verification workload runs at 3.240 J/hour on the ground, and the [ownership math](The-Replacement-Grade) is published both ways.)
- **Mass-market connectivity: substantially substitutable.** Fibre, fixed wireless and community-owned mesh serve the overwhelming majority of the subscriber base the constellations bill; for the *verdict layer* — gauges, warnings, court calls — the ground mesh delivers it with zero rent and zero reentry mass. (MEASURED for the verdict layer; the broadband scope refusal stands in capitals in the producing program.)
- **The genuine remainder: maritime, polar, disaster-cut and truly unreachable users.** This is the fraction of the service that is *not* currently deliverable from the ground at comparable capability, and this page does not pretend otherwise. (STATED AS THE REMAINDER — unquantified here; quantifying it is exactly the kind of measurement nobody is required to produce.)

**So the favours below are read at their true size: they are facts about the remainder, not licences for the whole.** The chlorine advantage is real *for launches that survive questions 1 and 2*. For the fully substitutable services — the million-satellite compute filing above all — the flourishing-first answer precedes chemistry: **the cleanest reentry is the one that does not happen, and for that service, it does not have to.**

## What cuts in SpaceX's favour, with the same weight

- **Their engines burn kerosene, which emits no chlorine** — and the sharpest recent analysis puts the dominant depletion driver at solid-propellant chlorine. On the axis that paper says matters most, this operator scores *well*. (CITED, NOT MEASURED — Barker 2026, carried as published. **Conditional per the ordering above:** a fact about launches that survive the necessity and substitutability questions, not a licence for the ones that do not.)
- **One peer-reviewed model finds the opposite sign** (a weaker ozone hole); another finds a small ozone *increase*. (CITED, NOT MEASURED — Maloney 2025; Revell 2025, whose alumina is launch alumina.)
- **The radiative term today is negligible: 0.1% of all greenhouse forcing — one part in 860.** It becomes large only at the filed, unbuilt scales. (PROJECTION over a MEASURED baseline — `reproduce/radiative-baseline.swift`.)
- **The most recent year is not continuing the climb.** 2026 to date annualises to 187.3 t/yr against 2025's 211.6 t — **0.89×, flat to slightly down** — and the 2026 mass share reads 44.5% against 2025's 45.8%. ## The impact statement

> **One operator has moved, in six years, from zero to nearly half of all catalogued human-made mass reentering Earth's atmosphere, and holds licences for a fleet whose replacement cycle projects human metal injection into the stratosphere to between an eighth and a quarter of the natural flux — with filings on record that project it to five to eleven times nature and to nearly half the radiative forcing of every greenhouse gas humanity has ever emitted. The biological consequence of that trajectory is not known, because the transfer functions have never been measured, and no law requires the forcing itself to be recorded. The harm to humanity is therefore not a prediction — it is an exposure: three planetary accounts moving at percent scale under an environmental review skipped by standing rule — a rule the government's own auditor has questioned at this scale ([grounded here](Reentry-Forcing-Nobody-Measures)) — graded NOT_KNOWN at the exact link where the ocean food web begins. We live here. So do they.**

## What would change this assessment

Stated so it can be falsified, not so it can be believed:

1. A **reentry emissions inventory** — mass, composition, ablation altitude, per object. Three years of it settles the sign disputes that five models currently cannot.
2. A **UV-B dose-response measurement for phytoplankton** at the projected regional doses. Standard marine biology; unfunded.
3. **Regional minima published alongside global means** by the existing models — one output field.
4. Any demonstration that the ablated fraction, the 12× regional factor, or the linear scaling breaks in the kind direction — each is flagged in the study as an assumption that could fail either way.

If those come back benign, this page gets a dated correction saying so, the way our other wrong numbers did. Until someone measures, the exposure stands, and it is not ours to accept on anyone's behalf — **including on behalf of the operator's own children, who live under the same sky.**
