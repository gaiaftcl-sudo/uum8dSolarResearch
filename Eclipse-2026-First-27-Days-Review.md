# Eclipse 2026 — The First 27 Days: Full Operational Review

*Written on T+1, 2026-08-13 — twenty-eight days after the experiment was born on 2026-07-16 and one day after the eclipse it was built to predict. All times are UT unless marked otherwise. Every number on this page was measured, and carries the date it was measured. This page follows the same rule as every other page in this wiki: a review softened is a review failed.*

## What failed and what held

This review covers the experiment's first twenty-seven days, complete and measurable: one afternoon of construction (2026-07-16), one morning of polish (2026-07-17), twenty-five days of near-total silence, the eclipse itself (2026-08-12), and the reckoning of the day after (2026-08-13, today). The work took real operational damage in that span, and this page itemizes all of it — the monitoring loop that went dark for twenty-three days *including eclipse day*, the service that was staged but never installed, the public promise of an August-13 verdict that the archive's own calendar made impossible, and a counting error written into the permanent record within three minutes of the data it miscounted.

The prediction stands apart from this review. It has not been graded yet, because the archive that grades it has not yet published eclipse day. The four sealed claims of 2026-07-16 — the detection law, the pre-registered station-by-station prediction, the historical trial that ended in a recorded failure, and the prediction extensions with their frozen thresholds — were all re-verified byte-for-byte from disk on 2026-08-13. Four out of four. Their fingerprints (`89afd825011f060e`, `c3e6841a206b8058`, `6fe49bee70289fee`, `b106d2926b1bf2cf`) are identical to the ones stamped twenty-eight days ago. Nothing sealed has moved. Nothing sealed *can* move to meet the data when it arrives.

So the framing of this review is precise: **the operational surface of the experiment failed, repeatedly and measurably; the scientific core of the experiment is intact, frozen, and awaiting judgment.** The sections below separate those two facts without letting either excuse the other.

## What the historical record returned

The experiment did not go into eclipse day untested. On birth day it was run backward across three eclipses and one geomagnetic storm — 82 station-day pairs in the [historical corpus](Eclipse-2026-Historical-Corpus.md), 16 positives and 66 negatives, every archive file carrying a content hash (100 archive rows, 18 distinct day-scans, all fetched and hashed 2026-07-16). The verdicts, frozen 2026-07-16 and unchanged since:

| Trial | Result under the frozen law | False positives |
|---|---|---|
| 2017-08-21 total eclipse (5 stations) | **5/5 detected**, couplings 327–560 permille | 0 |
| 2023-10-14 annular eclipse (4 stations) | **3/4 — one miss** | 2 (seasonal control edges, 2023-09-17) |
| 2024-04-08 total eclipse (7 stations + 21 controls) | **7/7 detected**, all controls null | 0 |
| Gannon storm 2024-05-10 (positive phase, 7 stations) | correctly null, 7/7 | 0 |
| Gannon storm 2024-05-11 (negative phase, 7 stations) | — | **7 — every station** |
| **Total** | **15/16** | **9** |

Two of those rows are the ones that matter most, because both are failures and both were published on the day they were found.

**The Eugene miss.** At Eugene, Oregon, on 2023-10-14, the law found nothing inside the eclipse gate (58,207–59,277 s UT). One genuine eclipse signature out of sixteen did not thread the detector. It was sealed as the named falsifier in Seal 3, published in the public trial table as "no projection," and then promoted into [Blind spots](Eclipse-2026-Blind-Spots.md) as Story 1 — a finding, not an embarrassment to bury. The closure of the historical trial is 15/16, and that is the number the seal carries.

**The Gannon false positive, which forced the finished discriminant.** On 2024-05-11 — the negative phase of the strongest geomagnetic storm in two decades — the frozen law fired at **all seven** stations. A detector that cannot tell an eclipse from a storm is not a detector. This failure forced the two-clause discriminant sealed later the same day (the "finished shear" of commit `a7a02d1f`, 18:55 UT): an eclipse signature must be **confined** (excess depletion in the window over the flanks) and **on time** (deepest bin inside the sealed lag gate). Under those two clauses, measured 2026-07-16 and re-derived exactly on 2026-08-13: the weakest true eclipse row shows an excess of **57,543 ppm** (Carbondale, 2024-04-08; the strongest is Carbondale 2017 at 315,376 ppm), while the strongest lag-passing storm row shows **42,836 ppm** (Dallas, 2024-05-11). Clean separation, gap **14,707 ppm**, threshold frozen at the midpoint, **50,189 ppm**. The storm rows are unmasked by both clauses at once: their raw window depletions are enormous (667,827–733,664 ppm) but unconfined — near-zero or negative excess (Mazatlan: −67,412 ppm) — and their deepest bins arrive outside the gate (−829 s, −1,027 s). Every storm pair the frozen law false-positived is rejected by confinement plus lag. That threshold went into Seal 4 *before* the eclipse, where it can no longer be tuned.

## The failure log

Every operational failure found in the evidence, worst first. Format: what happened; what it cost; what caught it; what changed.

**1. The watch went dark for twenty-three days — including the eclipse.** The live capture loop (Stage 3 of the pipeline) recorded its last sample at 18:01 UT on 2026-07-20 and recorded nothing again until the morning of 2026-08-13. The eclipse gate on 2026-08-12 — 16:43:40 to 19:22:06 UT across the five sealed stations — fell **entirely** inside the dark window. *Cost:* the promised live storm-gate watch (1-minute estimated Kp, continuously, 15:30–20:00 UT on eclipse day, per the [advisory](Eclipse-2026-Satellite-Aviation-Advisory.md)) never ran; if a storm had hit during the eclipse, the experiment would not have known in real time. *What caught it:* a T+1 measurement of the ledger — the per-day sample counts, which read 2,164 on 07-20 and zero for every day after. Not a guard, not an alert; a person looking, twenty-four days late. *What changed:* the eclipse-day storm gate was graded retrospectively from the definitive planetary index (fetched 2026-08-13 10:46 UT): maximum Kp **2.667** on 08-12, in eight three-hour bins of 1.7, 2.7, 2.0, 1.7, 2.3, 1.3, 2.3, 1.0 — nowhere near the ≥ 5 storm flag, comfortably under the sealed Kp ≤ 4 quiet rule. The science verdict survives on definitive data; the live-watch promise does not. The outage is published, dated, on the [runbook](Eclipse-2026-Operations-Runbook.md).

**2. The service that would have prevented failure 1 was never installed.** The watch loop's scheduler definition was written, committed to the branch on 2026-07-16 (19:30 UT, with its own dedicated binary), and staged — and the [runbook](Eclipse-2026-Operations-Runbook.md) recorded installation as a pending handoff, with a manual loop as fallback. The installation never happened: queried on 2026-08-13, the system scheduler returns "Could not find service." The manual fallback ran for four days and stopped on 07-20 with the operator's attention. *Cost:* the entire twenty-three-day outage — this is its mechanism. *What caught it:* the same T+1 audit, by direct query. *What changed:* capture was restarted 2026-08-13 (775 samples by 11:12 UT, accreting since). The staged definition still exists on the branch; it is still not installed. See item 11.

**3. The verdict was promised for August 13; the archive keeps a two-to-three-week calendar.** The [overview](Eclipse-2026-Overview.md) said "On August 13 the resolution runs against measured data and the verdict — pass or fail — lands in these same tables." The advisory said "publicly, on August 13." The runbook went further and asserted the mechanism: "Madrigal's definitive gridded TEC lands T+1." That mechanism claim was **factually wrong**. Measured on 2026-08-13 by probing the archive directly (twice): 2026-07-25 is posted, 2026-08-01 is not — the posting boundary sits at a **two-to-three-week lag**, and 2026-08-12 has not been published. *Cost:* a public date, missed publicly, on four pages. *What caught it:* the T+1 resolution attempt itself — the grading run reached for data that was not there. *What changed:* a dated resolution-status addendum shipped the same morning (2026-08-13, 07:05–07:06 local) on the [prediction registry](Eclipse-2026-Prediction-Registry.md), the runbook, the overview, and the advisory, stating the measured lag and the expected grading window (~2026-08-30). The addendum's own words: the sky kept the appointment; the archive has not yet.

**4. The permanent record overcounted the corpus by fourteen, three minutes after the data was final.** The corpus file received its last-ever write at 18:13:51 UT on 2026-07-16: 82 pairs. At 18:16:19 UT, commit `c35191f7`'s message claimed "96 fossil pairs." Two reconstructions fit the figure exactly, and neither is proven. The best fit: 82 = 16 positives + 66 negatives, where the 66 already include the 14 Gannon-storm pairs — counting those 14 again as their own category gives 96. The rival: 82 pairs plus the 14 Halloween-2003 station-day scans (2 days × 7 stations, ingested archive-only, never built into corpus rows) also gives 96. Both are reconstructions after the fact; the measured facts are only that the file held 82 at 18:13:51 UT, the message said 96 at 18:16:19 UT, and the file has not been modified since. *Cost:* a wrong figure in the immutable commit history, later copied into internal notes. No data was ever lost — the file's composition (20 + 20 + 42 pairs across 2017/2023/2024) has been byte-stable for 28 days. *What caught it:* the ledger-rendered pages themselves, which never repeated the prose figure — the trial table renders exactly 82 rows, the benchmark says "all 28 pairs," and the same-day visuals commit (20:30 UT) reads "(82 ledger rows)." The rendering pipeline draws from the ledger, not from commit messages, so the error could not propagate to the public surface. *What changed:* the correct accounting is now stated here, in the permanent record's counter-record — as reconstruction, not as measurement. The companion figure in the same sentence — "18 day-scans" — was measured **true**: 18 distinct hashed archive files, of which 15 became corpus rows (the two Halloween-2003 storm days were ingested archive-only, as the sealed note declared; one 2023 control day was ingested but never paired).

**5. The lag-window prose contradicted the sealed number.** Pages drafted on birth day described the arrival window as ±300 s; the sealed window in Seal 4 is **[−300, +1200] s** — asymmetric, because the measured bottomside delays run to +1,073 s. *Cost:* a few hours of internal inconsistency on birth day; had it shipped, the public text would have promised a stricter test than the sealed one — and the grading run would have exposed the mismatch. *What caught it:* a same-day cross-check of prose against sealed text. *What changed:* commit `c60d5b66`, 20:12 UT, 2026-07-16; verified on 2026-08-13 that every shipped page reads [−300, +1200] s.

**6. Blind-Spots Story 4 quoted the wrong offsets.** The story cited a deepest-bin offset of −383 s where the correct values were **−706 s and −646 s**. *Cost:* minutes — the fix (`fbc74c00`) landed at 19:17 UT on 2026-07-16, the same clock-hour the story was committed. *What caught it:* re-derivation of the story's numbers from the ledger before the public push. *What changed:* verified 2026-08-13 that the shipped page quotes −706 s and −646 s; the value −383 s survives in the record only as a legitimately different control-day row at Eugene, which is what made the misquote easy to make.

**7. A diagram label broke the renderer.** The advisory's timeline diagram used colons inside a milestone label, which the diagram syntax treats as structure; the diagram failed to render. *Cost:* one broken figure for about two hours on birth day. *What caught it:* looking at the rendered page rather than the source. *What changed:* commit `6e78ccfa`, 20:16 UT, 2026-07-16; the label now reads "totality peak off Iceland" with no colons. Logged because the rule is *every* failure, and because it is the smallest instance of the pattern in items 5 and 6: the shipped surface is what must be checked, not the intention behind it.

**8. Live-endpoint fetches carry no content hash.** Of 133 provenance rows measured 2026-08-13, the 100 archive rows each carry a real content hash (18 distinct day-files); the 33 rows from live endpoints (the planetary-index and magnetometer feeds) store a placeholder "-" instead. *Cost:* the live slice of the record is not independently re-verifiable the way the archive slice is; if a feed's history were revised upstream, the ledger could not prove what it originally saw. *What caught it:* the T+1 provenance audit. *What changed:* nothing yet — this stands as an open defect, stated here so it cannot be mistaken for a design choice.

**9. The corpus file itself is outside version control.** The 82-row historical corpus lives in one file — `~/.gaiaftcl/franklin/eclipse-fossils.jsonl` — and that file is not git-tracked on any branch: `git ls-files` and `git log --all -- '**/eclipse-fossils*'` both return empty (measured 2026-08-13). Only the Swift builder that writes it (`EclipseFossilCorpus.swift`) is tracked. Every "byte-stable for 28 days" and "untouched for 28 days" claim on this page therefore rests on a single filesystem mtime, with no version-control custody behind it. *Cost:* if the one untracked file were altered or lost, nothing could prove its original contents beyond the ledger-rendered trial tables. By this page's own standard — item 8 grades the un-hashed live-endpoint rows as not independently re-verifiable — this is the same class of defect, sitting under the very trial the seals cite. *What caught it:* the T+1 audit, by direct query of the version-control record. *What changed:* nothing yet — logged as a standing debt.

**10. The interim month's work bypassed the provenance chain.** Between 2026-07-23 and 07-25, ten pages — Study 07 (four pages), Study 08, Study 03's predictions, the white paper, the readers' guide, the comparison page, and the public README — were authored directly against the public repositories. They exist **nowhere** in the private source repository, on either branch. **11. The fix for failure 2 makes a new promise with the same defect.** Today's resolution-status addendum states that "a standing daily probe watches for the posting" of the eclipse-day archive. Measured 2026-08-13, hours after that sentence shipped: no scheduler entry for the probe exists — the promise currently rests on manual execution, the exact mechanism that went dark for twenty-three days. *Cost:* none yet; the grading window opens around 2026-08-30. *What caught it:* auditing today's own output by the same standard as the last month's. *What changed:* the promise is hereby graded **unverified until a scheduler artifact exists**. If the grading is late because nobody was watching, this item is where the blame was pre-registered.

## The fair test — conditions verified (added after the T+1 space-weather sweep)

What a quiet day gives the experiment, precisely, is a fair test. The storm-pivot gate existed for one failure mode — storm variability swamping the eclipse signal and making attribution impossible. It never fired. So whatever the ionosphere did along the totality path on 2026-08-12 is attributable to the eclipse with the smallest confounder set solar maximum allows. Every confounder was then verified against raw or definitive sources on 2026-08-13, and every verifying byte was SHA-sealed into the raw archive:

- **Flares: absent.** The GOES X-ray record for the 15:30–20:00 UT window holds exactly one event — a B5.4 (16:41–16:55 UT) that ended 43 minutes before the Iceland core window, with window-maximum long-band flux 5.6×10⁻⁷ W/m² — B-class throughout, zero C, M, or X from first contact to last. The forecast had put M-class probability near 1%; it verified true.
- **The pre-eclipse disturbance: milder than forecast, and outside the baselines by construction.** A filament-eruption arrival had been forecast for 2026-08-11 at up to Kp 5. Measured (GFZ definitive): Aug 9 max 2.667, Aug 10 max 2.000, Aug 11 max 3.000, eclipse day max 2.667. - **Solar wind: a mild residual tail, quantified.** The 1-minute L1 feed (captured on T+1, hours before its rolling window discarded eclipse day) shows proton speed 382–478 km/s and normal density through the eclipse window, with Bz between −5.0 and +3.4 nT and 56 minutes weakly southward — the low-Kp-but-stirred scenario present only in this mild, named form. **Why a storm would have voided the test, not hardened it.** The quiet-day rule is *inside* the seal: the law grades on Kp ≤ 4, full stop. A storm on August 12 would not have been a harder test — it would have been no test, the prediction ungradable under its own frozen terms. And the storm question did not go unanswered; it was answered in July, with better storm data than luck could ever have scheduled. The Gannon superstorm — the strongest in two decades, fourteen station-days of it — is in the corpus: the law fired false on all seven stations of its negative phase, the failure was published raw, and it forced the confinement-plus-lag discriminant that then rejected every storm row — depletions near 700,000 ppm, all unconfined, all off-schedule, all correctly thrown out. Eclipse-versus-storm was settled twenty-seven days before the eclipse; August 12 did not need to host a rematch. What a storm on the day would actually have done is corrupt both halves of the prediction at once: every totality detection becomes "the storm did it," and the null stations — whose *silence* is half the claim — light up with storm-driven electron content that has nothing to do with the Moon. Pass gets disputed; miss gets excused.
