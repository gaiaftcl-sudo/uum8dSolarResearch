### 2.2 The arithmetic underneath — no supercomputer, no floats, and why that changes what a verdict is

There is a fact about this experiment that the tables do not show: **none of it was computed the way modern geophysics is normally computed.** No supercomputer. No floating point anywhere a claim lives. Every sealed number — obscuration in parts-per-million, time in whole UT seconds, depletion as integer ppm of baseline, thresholds like 50,189 — exists on a whole-integer lattice, and every verdict is an integer comparison. The law never asks whether 0.5754 exceeds 0.5019 in some machine's rounding; it asks whether 57,543 ≥ 50,189, and that question has exactly one answer on every computer ever built.

**A floating-point verdict is a property of a machine. An integer verdict is a property of the numbers.** Two computers running the same float pipeline can disagree in the last bits, and at a decision boundary the last bit *is* the verdict; a tolerance is a place where an argument lives. Integer arithmetic has no last bit to argue about: the grading can be re-run by a stranger, on any machine, in any decade, and must produce **byte-identical** output. That is why four seals re-verified **byte-for-byte, 28 days after stamping, 4/4** — a sentence that cannot be written about a float pipeline. The honesty architecture rests on arithmetic that cannot drift.

**No supercomputer was involved, and that is the point.** The full experiment runs on one desk machine reading public archives. The scarce resource was never float throughput; it was **exactness and discipline**, and those are free. The lattice touches the world at exactly one guarded crossing — a single quantization step (ppm, milli-units, whole seconds) — and from there no approximate number touches the record. (Stated honestly: the T+1 first look was an exploratory calculation on the custody bytes; the sealed grading that supersedes it runs entirely on the integer law.)

**What actually changes:** on conventional arithmetic a verdict is a computation you are asked to trust; on the lattice it is a **fact you can check** — the same fact on every machine, for every person, at every time. That is what let a fully public operation seal a claim twenty-seven days ahead and hand the grading to anyone.
