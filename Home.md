# Every season, about fifty tonnes of satellite burns up in the sky above your children.

Nobody is required to measure it. Nobody does.

That sentence is not a slogan and it is not a projection. It is a count. In calendar year 2025, **1,907 catalogued objects re-entered the atmosphere carrying 461,213,509 grams of dry mass**. Divide the year into its four seasons and roughly **fifty-three tonnes** of spacecraft turned to metal vapour in the stratosphere during each of them. Not on the ground, where it could be swept up. In the layer of air that holds the ozone.

You can check that number yourself in about four minutes. It is at the bottom of this page. It requires no account, no key, no permission, and no trust in us.

---

## What this page is

Two things, and they are connected.

**The first is a warning we can support with arithmetic**, about what happens to the sky if the filed satellite constellations get built. It is not an extinction claim. We tested that claim and it did not survive. What survives is worse in a quieter way: a slope, not a cliff, and no instrument pointed at it.

**The second is what we built instead**, because complaining is not a contribution. [Affine.Earth](The-Exactness-Seam) is a substrate where a verdict is an exact integer rather than a floating-point approximation, so that checking an answer is a **re-derivation** instead of a re-run on someone else's hardware. That sounds like a narrow technical preference. It is the entire difference between a number you can argue with and a number you have to take on faith.

The connection is this: the reason five research groups looked at the same satellites and came back **disagreeing about which direction the effect points** is not that the atmosphere is mysterious. Part of it is that their instruments do not agree with each other on arithmetic. We found one of those disagreements and closed it by counting. It took no atmospheric model at all.

## The story, season by season

Here is the honest version, told the way it will actually be experienced — which is slowly, and mostly without anyone noticing.

### This season, and last season, and the one before

**52.9 tonnes** per season. About half a tonne a day. This is happening right now, at the current rate, with the fleet that is already up there. Most of it is aluminium, which oxidises into alumina particles small enough to stay aloft for years.

Nobody has to report it. There is no reentry emissions inventory. No agency collects one. When the FCC authorised these constellations it did so under a **categorical exclusion** from environmental review — meaning the environmental review was formally skipped, not conducted and passed.

What you would feel this season: nothing. That is the point.

### The seasons after the authorised fleet is built

**477.3 tonnes per season.** Nine times today's rate. This is not a forecast about what companies might want — it is what the **19,408 satellites already authorised by the FCC** produce when they reach their five-year replacement cycle. The authorisation exists. The hardware is being built.

Five-year replacement is the part people miss. These are not monuments. A constellation of N satellites on a five-year life is a permanent **N/5 per year** disposal stream, forever, as long as the service exists. The mass never stops arriving because the fleet is always being replaced.

What you would feel: still nothing directly. What instruments *might* see, on the one published projection we could scale honestly, is a global column ozone reduction of about **0.26%**. Small. Detectable only if someone is looking, and the funding to look has not been appropriated.

### The seasons after the filed fleet is built

**2,460 tonnes per season.** Twenty-seven tonnes a day. This is the Gen3 filing — 100,000 satellites — a number that exists on paper at the FCC and has not been authorised.

The scaled projection here is **1.3% global column ozone loss**. For scale: the ozone loss that caused the world to negotiate the Montreal Protocol, sign it, and restructure a global refrigeration industry around it, peaked near **5%**.

We are now at roughly a quarter of the thing humanity treated as an emergency — from one industry, filed but not built, with no measurement requirement attached.

### The seasons after the orbital data centres

**20,000 tonnes per season. 219 tonnes every single day.**

This is the one that changed our assessment. In 2026 SpaceX filed for a constellation of roughly a million satellites carrying AI compute — data centres in orbit. Their own filing puts **40,000 units per year into disposal reentry**. We used their number, not a worse one we could have picked.

The scaled projection is **10.8% global column ozone loss** — twice the Montreal peak, sustained, from a single commercial programme, with no monitoring obligation.

And this one takes something else with it. Applying a published elemental analysis of a GPU to that same disposal rate, each year the following leaves the Earth system permanently, as vapour:

| material | per year, at the filed disposal rate |
|---|---|
| copper | ~1,000 tonnes |
| titanium | 20+ tonnes |
| bismuth | 20+ tonnes |
| palladium | 2+ tonnes |
| silver | ~2 tonnes |
| gold | ~170 kg |
| thallium | ~76 kg |

Palladium and thallium at that rate are roughly **one percent of annual global mine production** — dug out of the ground, lifted to orbit, and then deliberately vaporised rather than recycled. There is no other industry on Earth permitted to dispose of critical minerals by atmospheric injection.

### What it actually looks like when it arrives

Not a cliff. A slope, and slopes are politically survivable in a way that cliffs are not.

More UV at ground level means more skin cancer and more cataracts, spread across decades and across a whole population, so it shows up as a shifted statistic and never as an event. Crop yields move a few percent. Phytoplankton — the bottom of the ocean food web and a serious share of atmospheric oxygen production — is UV-sensitive, and a few percent there propagates upward slowly.

Nothing on that list produces a headline on the day it starts. Every item on it is measurable in advance if anyone measures. **Nobody is measuring.**

## The part where we tell you what we could not prove

We were pushed hard, by our own founder, to publish a specific catastrophic finding: a calculated year by which the stratosphere saturates and the situation becomes irreversible.

**We refused, and here is why**, because you should hold us to the same standard we are holding everyone else to.

The saturation calculation requires a threshold concentration at which the atmosphere's behaviour changes. We could not find one. When we worked the arithmetic backwards, the threshold the calculation needed sat at roughly the **ambient background level** — meaning the same formula applied to the pre-industrial sky, before any rocket ever flew, returns "already saturated." A model that says the sky was saturated in 1750 is not measuring the sky. It is measuring an error in the model.

So there is no year on this page. **We do not know when.** Anyone who gives you a date for this — including us, including anyone citing us — is giving you a number that does not exist yet.

We also went looking for facts that cut *against* alarm, and we publish them with the same weight:

- **One of the five models reports the opposite sign.** Maloney et al. 2025 finds a *weaker* springtime ozone hole under satellite ablation. It is a real paper by real scientists and it disagrees with our concern.
- **Revell et al. 2025 reports at most a +0.27% ozone *increase*.**
- **The dominant chlorine source in the sharpest recent analysis is launch, not reentry** — and it comes from solid propellant. Kerosene emits no chlorine. Which means the operator we are most worried about scores *well* on that particular axis. Their engines are cleaner on the metric that paper says matters most.
- **The widely-quoted "28× divergence" between two scenarios is not a real disagreement.** We found it is a ratio between two different quantities in different units — one a reentry mass flux, one an alumina mass. Corrected to one set of units it is **3.2×**, and the two papers land within 4% of each other. Found by counting. No model was run.

That last one is the tell. A field where a five-fold error can sit unremarked in the literature for two years is not a field whose reassurances you should accept, and it is also not a field whose alarms you should accept. **Including ours.**

We removed a compound factor of **240×** of double-counting from our own first estimate, by counting: a units artifact (8.75×), a regulatory-tier conflation where we had mixed authorised with merely requested satellites (2.16×), a disposal-fraction error where we had assumed every satellite reenters when the filing says 4% do (5×), and a per-unit mass error (2.54×). Our first number was 240 times too scary. **We publish that, because a warning that will not correct itself is propaganda.**

## So what do we actually want

Not a ban. Three things, all boring, all cheap:

**1. Measure it.** A mandatory reentry emissions inventory, the way there is one for aircraft. Mass, composition, altitude of ablation, per object. This costs approximately nothing and it does not exist. Every argument on this page — ours and theirs — would be settled inside three years by data nobody is currently collecting.

**2. End the categorical exclusion.** Not "reject the constellations." Review them. The exclusion means the environmental question was never asked, and that is a procedural fact, not an opinion about the answer.

**3. Put the compute on the ground.** Which brings us to the alternative.

We are going to be scrupulous here, because this is the section where a pitch usually appears and we do not want you to read one.

The comparison below is honest for the **verification** workload — deciding whether an answer is correct — and it is **not** honest for model training. ### The row that actually matters

Both approaches produce numbers that people will act on — that regulators will cite, that companies will build against, that end up in someone's policy document.

Only one of them produces a number **a stranger can re-derive without trusting the party that produced it.**

That is not an efficiency argument. It is what makes a verdict *evidence* instead of *testimony*. The five disagreeing atmospheric models are the demonstration: five groups, one physical question, answers that disagree on sign, and no shared arithmetic on which to settle it. When the instruments cannot be reconciled, the loudest institution wins the argument by default. That is the failure mode this substrate exists to remove.

### What "post-float" means, concretely

Floating-point arithmetic is not wrong — it is *approximate*, and the approximation depends on your hardware, your compiler version, and the order the sum happened in. Three major engineering vendors document in their own manuals that their results are not bit-reproducible across platforms. The relevant digital-twin standard, ISO 23247-1, contains **zero occurrences** of the word reproducibility.

So when two parties disagree about a simulated result, there is no procedure to settle it. There is only escalation.

Exact rational arithmetic removes that. A verdict is an integer over an integer. Two machines either produce the same one or they do not, and if they do not, exactly one of them is broken and it is findable. Across our own 48-domain mesh, each domain declares three exact rationals satisfying:

> **entropy_bare − entropy_delta = entropy_resolved = 1/1**

The continuous form of a law carries excess structural entropy, always greater than one. Adopting the exact form removes precisely Δ. What remains is unity — one answer, replayable by anyone, on any machine. **Nine of forty-eight domains currently satisfy it.** The other thirty-nine are work in progress and are listed as such.

The atmosphere domain does **not** satisfy it, and we say so on its page. The forcing side resolves to 1/1 by counting. The response side is stuck at 6/1, and counting cannot finish it — because the residual is not a harder sum, it is **a measurement nobody has taken.**
