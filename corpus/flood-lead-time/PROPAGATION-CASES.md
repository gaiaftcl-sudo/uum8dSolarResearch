# Lead time after detection — three measured cases

**This is evidence, not a study.** No sealed adversary, no float-vs-exact comparison. It
is the worked material for the lives lane of the business justification and the
applications section of the sovereign-pod charter.

**The program claims LEAD TIME AFTER DETECTION. It does not claim prediction, and it does
not claim lives saved.** Current science cannot forecast when a given slope will fail or
when a given cell will drop its rain. What is establishable is the interval between a
hazard becoming detectable and its arrival downstream, and that interval is arithmetic
once the channel is known. Every figure below is that interval or an input to it.

---

## Case 1 — Guadalupe River, Texas, 4 July 2025

Corpus pinned in `corpus/`, sha256 in `corpus/SHA256SUMS`. USGS NWIS, parameter 00065,
public and anonymous. Stage as exact integer milli-feet — the served values carry two
decimals, so the conversion is a string split and a multiply and no float parse enters.

Against a threshold frozen at 10.000 ft, stated before the test:

    Hunt      crosses 10.000 ft at 03:00
    Kerrville crosses 10.000 ft at 06:00        LEAD TIME 180 MINUTES
    Kerrville's own stage when Hunt crossed:  1.470 ft

Peak-to-peak propagation: Hunt → Kerrville **+95 min**, → Comfort **+350 min**, → Spring
Branch **+1415 min**. Hunt rose 19.350 ft in 95 minutes, peaking at 444 milli-ft/min
(26.640 ft/hour); Kerrville peaked at 885 milli-ft/min (53.100 ft/hour) — the wave
**steepened** as it travelled.

**The Hunt record ends at its own maximum.** A 35-minute gap from 04:35 (29.450 ft) to a
single final reading of 37.520 ft at 05:10, then nothing.

## Case 2 — Nepal and Tibet, 26 August 2026

An ice-and-rock mass of roughly 0.2 km², a glacier section over 600 m wide, detached at
about 5,200 m near Langtang Lirung and fell approximately 1,200 m. Sources place the
detachment variously on the Nepal side and in the Lhende catchment on the Tibet side;
Petley gives 28.2765°N, 85.5194°E, effectively on the border. **Not a glacial lake
outburst flood and not earthquake-triggered** (Petley, Landslide Blog / Eos; USGS).
Frictional heating melted the ice, the mass entrained sediment and river water, and the
resulting debris flow briefly dammed the Lhende Khola before releasing.

**The propagation, and this is the load-bearing measurement for the whole lives lane:**

| quantity | value | source |
|---|---|---|
| seismic signal radiated by the collapse | equivalent **M5.2** | USGS, confirmed generated by the collapse |
| debris-flow front speed | **~50 m/s (180 km/h)** | Eos / The Conversation |
| Gyirong Port destroyed after the seismic event | **7 minutes** | surveillance-camera timestamp |
| settlements struck along | **72 km** reach | Nepal NDRRMA |
| damage traced downstream | **≥100 km** | — |
| Galchhi gauge rise | **~9 m in 30 minutes** | ICIMOD via Reuters |

**Seismic waves travel at ~6 km/s. The debris flow travelled at ~0.05 km/s. The signal
outran the water by a factor of roughly 120.** The USGS had the signal on the day; what
lagged was interpretation — it was first read as a tectonic earthquake.

**The four reported failure modes are the mesh case, stated by others:**

1. **Wrong instrument for the hazard.** Water-level monitors were optimised for monsoon
   and perennial floods. A gauge that records a catastrophic rise has already spent the
   lead time.
2. **Sensors destroyed by what they were measuring.** Upstream monitoring stations were
   swept away before alerts could be sent.
3. **Transboundary blind spot.** Source and upstream infrastructure sit across a border;
   Nepal's DHM and the China Meteorological Administration agreed to strengthen real-time
   sharing only after the event.
4. **Altitude.** Installation and maintenance above 5,000 m is confined to short summer
   windows, so coverage is sparse.

**Where a relay existed, minutes were actionable.** A school headteacher who received
relayed upstream warnings evacuated roughly 900 children about **ten minutes** before
impact.

**And the lead time is officially stated as achievable on that same channel.** A residual
barrier lake of ~2 million m³ formed at the Chhochen Khola / Purepu Tsangpo confluence.
Chinese authorities now monitor it with satellites, radar and drones and state they
believe they can deliver **approximately 30 minutes of warning** if it deteriorates. The
constraint was instrumentation and cross-border relay, not physics.

Officially reported, preliminary and still rising at time of search: Nepal NDRRMA **903
deaths, 2,498 missing** (589 foreign nationals), 1,473 injured, as of 30–31 August 2026.
China CCTV, Gyirong County: **16 deaths, 546 missing**, as of 29 August 2026.

A Nature news item is headlined "Early warning could have saved hundreds of lives in Nepal
floods". **That is that outlet's framing and is recorded here as such, not as an
established finding, and this program does not adopt it.**

## Case 3 — Chamoli, Uttarakhand, 7 February 2021 — the peer-reviewed number

Not Tibet, and carried here for one reason: it is the only case with a **published,
peer-reviewed measurement of the exact quantity this program claims.**

About 27 million m³ of rock and ice fell from the north face of Ronti Peak; frictional
heating melted nearly all the ice into a debris flow (Shugar et al., *Science*, 2021,
doi:10.1126/science.abh4455). Measured front velocities: **~25 m/s at 15 km**, **~16 m/s
at 26 km**. More than 200 dead or missing.

A subsequent study of the regional seismic network quantified the warning potential
retrospectively from the collapse's seismic signature alone: **~10 minutes at 13 km and
~14 minutes at 19.7 km** (*Earth Systems and Environment*, doi:10.1007/s41748-023-00364-y).

## Case 4 — the recurrence argument: Rasuwagadhi, 8 July 2025

Fourteen months before the 2026 event, **in the same valley**, a previously unnoticed
supraglacial lake on the Purepu Glacier in Tibet, at ~5,150 m and ~36 km upstream of
Rasuwagadhi, drained suddenly. At least 9 dead, 19 missing; the Friendship Bridge and the
Rasuwagadhi hydropower project were destroyed. Attributed by Nepal's Department of
Hydrology and Meteorology and ICIMOD.

**The lake was visible growing in satellite imagery between December 2024 and June 2025,
and was unmonitored.** Same channel, twice in fourteen months, with the precursor sitting
in archive data nobody was reading. That is the argument for a *fixed* mesh on a known
corridor rather than for better forecasting.

---

## The convergence, and it is the mesh case

**In two independent disasters on two continents, the instrument died in the event it was
measuring.** The Hunt gauge's record ends at its own maximum. Nepal's upstream stations
were swept away before alerts could be sent.

This is not a criticism of either gauge network. A gauge in a channel is exposed by
design, and both were doing exactly what they were built to do. It is a statement about
**topology**: a single instrument on a channel is a single point of failure, and it fails
at the moment of maximum information value. Losing one node of a mesh costs one node.

## What none of this establishes

That any outcome would have been different. That is not derivable from a stage record, a
camera timestamp or a seismogram, and this program does not assert it. Nothing here is a
claim about the National Weather Service, Nepal's DHM, the China Meteorological
Administration, or any decision taken on any of these days.

What is establishable, and is the whole of the lives lane: **the interval existed, it was
measurable, and in three of the four cases it was not converted into a relay.**
